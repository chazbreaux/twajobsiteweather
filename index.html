About this data

Each day’s record fills in as it happens, at about 08:00, 12:00 and 17:00 Central time, and only for sites marked for history. Overnight rain covers the previous day from 17:01 to 08:00. Conditions are the average cloud cover for the period (Sunny, Partly cloudy or Cloudy), or Rain if any rain fell during it. The past-24-hour rain total is a NOAA radar-and-gauge estimate for each site’s location (via Iowa State’s Mesonet); a + after it means the latest hour is still being processed, and when it is unavailable the page shows a weather-model total instead. The history log uses the same radar estimate for its rain amounts and for the Rain call; a value with a + is still missing its latest radar hour or two and may rise a little, and a value with a * is a weather-model estimate shown only until the first radar hours arrive. The log re-checks the last three days, so partial values are finalized and late or revised radar data is picked up. Temperatures and cloud cover come from the weather model. In the downloadable CSV, the columns after the blank one are the weather-model rain amounts for comparison. None of these are gauge readings, so they are estimates. The radar hides its faintest echoes (the light-blue and bright-green returns, roughly under 25 dBZ), because on clear nights these are usually insects, birds or ground clutter rather than rain. On quiet nights, when no site is raining or expected to rain and no storm is in view, it also hides the mid-green returns (roughly 25 to 30 dBZ). As soon as any site is raining, or rain is expected within about two hours, the lighter echoes come back so light rain still shows. Warnings and watches come from the National Weather Service and refresh every 2 minutes. Storm tracks come from the National Weather Service radar storm-tracking data (via Iowa State’s Mesonet) and mark radar-detected rotation and its projected path; a rotation or tornado-signature flag is a radar indication, not a confirmed tornado, and some storms are missed or false-alarmed. Future radar (optional checkbox) is an educated guess for the next 90 minutes: the latest real radar slid along the direction the storms are moving, so it assumes they keep the same speed and neither grow nor fade. When storm movement is not available it falls back to a NOAA weather-model forecast (HRRR via Iowa State’s Mesonet). Either way it is a projection, not real radar, and it is least reliable for new storms forming or fading. Times on the radar loop are Central time. All of this is a convenience, not a substitute for official alerts, so always follow local emergency instructions. The page refreshes itself, so it can stay open on a screen.
